Step 1: How to Format a Script for Script-to-Video AI

Unlike traditional text-to-video tools that only animate one isolated 5-second prompt, script to video AI understands narrative structure, character turns, and multi-shot pacing. To get studio-grade results, format your page with three clean elements:

  • Scene Slugline: State interior/exterior, location, and time of day (e.g., INT. PENTHOUSE BOARDROOM - NIGHT).
  • Visual Action Beat (Present Tense): Describe only what the camera can see and hear—avoid internal thoughts that cannot be filmed.
  • Tight Dialogue Blocks: Keep spoken lines punchy (5 to 15 words per cut) so facial expressions and lip-sync stay natural.

Step 2: Breaking a Script Into Consistent Shots

Great vertical dramas and short films rely on shot-reverse-shot editing rather than long, static master shots. Break each scene beat into alternating camera setups:

  1. Establishing Wide Shot (3–4s): Grounds the viewer in the location and lighting mood.
  2. Over-the-Shoulder / Medium Shot (4–5s): Delivers the primary line of dialogue while showing body language.
  3. Close-Up Reaction Cut (3–4s): Captures subtle eye movement or tension before cutting to the next beat.
